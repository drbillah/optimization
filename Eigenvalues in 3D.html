The signs of the eigenvalues decide the shape of xᵀAx

For a symmetric 2×2 matrix, x = (x₁, x₂) is one vector and xᵀAx is one number. Plot that number as height over every x and you get one surface.

xᵀAx = a·x₁² + 2b·x₁x₂ + c·x₂² = λ₁y₁² + λ₂y₂²

In eigen-coordinates y = Vᵀx (a rotation), the cross term disappears. Each eigenvalue is the bend along its own eigenvector: one positive and one negative gives a saddle.

Cutting a bowl at height h gives an ellipse

The plane z = h meets the bowl where xᵀAx = h. That set of vectors is an ellipse; its shadow is drawn dashed on the floor.

Its half-width along vᵢ is √(h/λᵢ), so it is narrowest along the steepest direction. Make the matrix a saddle and the same cut becomes a hyperbola.

With x = (x₁, x₂, x₃) the floor is already 3D, so the graph of xᵀAx lives in 4D. The figure shows the slice xᵀAx = ±1: the 3D version of a level curve.

As a relationship matrix, A predicts what two animals do after a disturbance

Let x be how far each population sits above or below its balance level. Near balance, dx/dt = Ax, where aᵢⱼ is the effect of species j on species i's growth. Diagonal entries are self-limitation.

d‖x‖²/dt = 2·xᵀAx

So the quadratic form is the rate the disturbance grows. Negative eigenvalues: it dies out. A positive one: it runs away along that eigenvector. Complex: the populations cycle.
